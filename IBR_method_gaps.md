# Methods proven elsewhere, not yet applied to the Indo-Burma Ranges

17 approaches standard on other complex margins, checked against 895 regional publications.
10 need nothing but data already in hand.

Ranked 1-17, most startable first. The order scores each opening on what could begin with the
archive and one GPU already in hand, how novel the regional result would be, and how much the
answer would matter, with ties broken towards work that unlocks the items below it. It assumes
no field budget and no ship time: ranks 14-17 are held back by money and permissions, not by
science. It is a judgement, not a measurement.

## 01. Modern b-value estimators with significance testing

**Track:** AI / ML · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** archive

- **Why this rank:** Laptop-scale, published catalogues only, and it lands squarely inside your own thesis topic. Mirwald et al. (2024) is already in your library and the 28 regional b-value papers in this bibliography are the ready-made test set. The most result per week of work on this list.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Mizrahi et al. (2024, SRL) — in your library — showed that most published spatial b-value variations fail a significance test, and introduced b-positive and the b-significant framework.
- **Why it fits here:** Every one of the 28 b-value papers in this bibliography uses maximum-curvature or MLE with no significance testing. Re-analysing the NE India b-value literature with these estimators is a direct, publishable methodological correction of your own field.
- **What you need:** Published catalogues only. This is a laptop project with a high citation ceiling.
- **Read these:**
    - Mirwald et al. 2024 — *Seismological Research Letters* — [10.1785/0220240190](https://doi.org/10.1785/0220240190)
    - van der Elst 2021 — *Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth* — [10.1029/2020jb021027](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020jb021027)

## 02. Deep-learning phase picking and association over the whole NE India archive

**Track:** Seismology · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** archive

- **Why this rank:** The catalogue that ranks 3, 6 and 8 are all built on. Archive plus one GPU, no permits, no fieldwork — and Yang et al. (2024) already proved the doubling on the Myanmar side of the same structure.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Yang et al. (2024, GRL) ran a deep-learning picker on a temporary network in central Myanmar and recovered twice the events of standard processing, resolving activity on the Kabaw Fault.
- **Why it fits here:** Your library already holds every manual — PhaseNet, EQTransformer, SeisBench, GaMMA, PyOcto — and not one has been turned on the Indian side. Regional catalogues still start near Mc 3.5; a DL catalogue typically drops Mc by a full unit and sharpens fault planes.
- **What you need:** The continuous waveform archive you already have, plus one GPU. No fieldwork, no permissions.
- **Read these:**

## 03. Matched-filter / template-matching catalogue extension

**Track:** Seismology · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** archive

- **Why this rank:** Runs on the same archive using your existing catalogue as templates, needs CPU rather than GPU, and gives an independent cross-check on the deep-learning catalogue instead of a second version of it.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Standard practice in Japan and California for two decades — and in your own library it has been applied to Mars (2026 InSight marsquake catalogue) but never to the Indo-Burma Ranges.
- **Why it fits here:** Kopili and Churachandpur–Mao need dense microseismicity before their fault planes can be drawn. Template matching finds events 1–2 magnitude units below the network threshold using the events you already catalogued as templates.
- **What you need:** Existing catalogue as templates + continuous data. Pairs naturally with the DL catalogue above.
- **Read these:**
    - Gibbons et al. 2006 — *Geophysical Journal International* — [10.1111/j.1365-246x.2006.02865.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-246x.2006.02865.x)
    - Shelly et al. 2007 — *Nature* — [10.1038/nature05666](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05666)
    - Ross et al. 2019 — *Science* — [10.1126/science.aaw6888](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaw6888)

## 04. A search for slow slip and tectonic tremor

**Track:** Seismology · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** archive

- **Why this rank:** The highest ceiling here and genuinely first-of-its-kind — but a null result is a real possibility, so it ranks below work that is certain to yield a paper. Best run alongside 1–3, not instead of them.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Cascadia (Rogers & Dragert 2003), Nankai, Hikurangi and Guerrero all host episodic tremor and slip on locked, shallow-dipping megathrusts.
- **Why it fits here:** Steckler et al. (2016, Nature Geoscience) showed the Indo-Burman megathrust is locked and loading with exactly that geometry. Every comparable margin that has been searched has found ETS. Nobody has searched here — a CrossRef query returns zero regional studies.
- **What you need:** Continuous GNSS (already recording) for transients, plus broadband envelope cross-correlation for tremor. A genuinely first-of-its-kind result if it exists.
- **Read these:**
    - Rogers et al. 2003 — *Science* — [10.1126/science.1084783](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1084783)
    - Obara 2002 — *Science* — [10.1126/science.1070378](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1070378)
    - Wallace et al. 2016 — *Science* — [10.1126/science.aaf2349](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf2349)

## 05. Ambient-noise monitoring of velocity change (dv/v)

**Track:** Seismology · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** archive

- **Why this rank:** Retrospective, archive-only and low-risk: it works on data already recorded. The monsoon-loading and Kopili pairing is half-written in the regional literature already.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Brenguier and co-workers established dv/v monitoring at Parkfield and Merapi; Lecocq et al. (2017), in your library, used it to track groundwater storage over 30 years.
- **Why it fits here:** Assam's monsoon hydrological loading and the Kopili fault are an ideal pairing — seasonal pore-pressure modulation of seismicity is measurable, and no one has tried it in the region.
- **What you need:** Two or more continuously recording broadbands and a noise-correlation pipeline. Retrospective — works on archived data.
- **Read these:**
    - Brenguier et al. 2008 — *Science* — [10.1126/science.1160943](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1160943)
    - Brenguier et al. 2008 — *Nature Geoscience* — [10.1038/ngeo104](https://doi.org/10.1038/ngeo104)
    - Lecocq et al. 2017 — *Scientific Reports* — [10.1038/s41598-017-14468-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-14468-9)

## 06. Self-supervised denoising of regional waveforms

**Track:** AI / ML · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** archive

- **Why this rank:** Cheap, archive-only, and it multiplies the yield of ranks 2 and 3 — but standing alone it reads as a methods note rather than a headline result.
- **Proven elsewhere:** DeepDenoiser and successors routinely recover signal from stations with poor SNR.
- **Why it fits here:** NE India station noise is a documented, well-characterised problem — much of your library is about exactly that. Nobody has applied learned denoising to it, and it multiplies the yield of every other method on this list.
- **What you need:** Archive waveforms plus a GPU. Natural companion to the DL picker.
- **Read these:**
    - Zhu et al. 2019 — *IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing* — [10.1109/tgrs.2019.2926772](https://doi.org/10.1109/tgrs.2019.2926772)

## 07. Time-dependent and operational earthquake forecasting

**Track:** Hazard · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** archive

- **Why this rank:** Published catalogues plus a declustering choice. It extends the b-value work directly, and every PSHA in this bibliography is still Poissonian, so the correction is easy to motivate.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Italy runs an operational earthquake forecast; New Zealand runs time-dependent hazard with renewal models.
- **Why it fits here:** Every PSHA in this bibliography is Poissonian — memoryless. For a margin with a documented 1897 M8.1 and a strain budget, renewal and ETAS models are the appropriate tool and nobody has fitted them.
- **What you need:** Existing catalogues plus a declustering choice. Directly extends the b-value work already being done.
- **Read these:**
    - Marzocchi et al. 2014 — *Seismological Research Letters* — [10.1785/0220130219](https://doi.org/10.1785/0220130219)

## 08. Graph neural networks for association and declustering

**Track:** AI / ML · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** archive

- **Why this rank:** Sound and doable, but it needs the dense catalogue from rank 2 to exist first. Sequence it after, not in parallel.
- **Proven elsewhere:** GNN associators and ML declustering now outperform nearest-neighbour and window methods on dense catalogues.
- **Why it fits here:** Once a DL catalogue exists for NE India it will contain far more events than classical declustering handles well — and declustering choice propagates straight into every b-value and hazard number in this bibliography.
- **What you need:** The catalogue you build in step one.
- **Read these:**
    - McBrearty et al. 2023 — *Bulletin of the Seismological Society of America* — [10.1785/0120220182](https://doi.org/10.1785/0120220182)

## 09. Machine learning for ionospheric TEC anomaly detection

**Track:** AI / ML · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** archive

- **Why this rank:** Public data and laptop-scale, and honest false-alarm accounting would be a genuine correction — but the precursor field itself is contested, which caps how far the result travels.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Global seismo-ionospheric work has moved to LSTM and autoencoder anomaly detection with proper false-alarm accounting.
- **Why it fits here:** All eight TEC papers here still use fixed 2σ thresholds over a sliding window. That method cannot quantify its own false-alarm rate — which is precisely the criticism levelled at earthquake-precursor claims. An ML treatment with honest skill scores would be a real contribution.
- **What you need:** IGS/GNSS TEC archives, all public.

## 10. Tsunami inundation modelling for the Bengal coast

**Track:** Hazard · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** compute

- **Why this rank:** Open-source tools, modest compute and enormous societal weight — but it sits outside your seismological core and wants a bathymetry and rupture-scenario collaborator.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Standard practice for every other subduction margin after 2004.
- **Why it fits here:** Cummins (2007) raised the giant-tsunami scenario for the northern Bay of Bengal nineteen years ago. There is still no inundation study for the Bangladesh–Myanmar coastline — one of the most densely populated, lowest-lying coasts on the planet.
- **What you need:** A rupture scenario, bathymetry and a shallow-water solver. All open-source.
- **Read these:**
    - Cummins 2007 — *Nature* — [10.1038/nature06088](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06088)

## 11. Exposure, fragility and risk — not just hazard

**Track:** Hazard · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** archive

- **Why this rank:** No new instruments needed in principle, yet the building inventory is months of survey and remote sensing, and the modelling is structural engineering rather than seismology.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Nath and co-workers built a full vulnerability and risk model for Kolkata (2015, in your library).
- **Why it fits here:** The NE India literature stops at hazard: PGA maps and source zones. No comparable exposure inventory or fragility-based loss model exists for Guwahati, Imphal, Shillong or Silchar, so the hazard numbers never convert into decisions.
- **What you need:** Building inventories (remote sensing plus survey) and published fragility curves.
- **Read these:**
    - Nath et al. 2015 — *Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences* — [10.5194/nhess-15-1103-2015](https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-15-1103-2015)

## 12. A 3-D basin velocity model and physics-based ground-motion simulation

**Track:** Hazard · **Status:** partial · **Cost:** compute

- **Why this rank:** High value and directly hazard-relevant, but constructing the velocity model is itself a multi-year project before a single simulation runs.
- **Proven elsewhere:** SCEC's CyberShake for California and NIED's work for the Kanto basin simulate ground motion from 3-D structure rather than empirical attenuation.
- **Why it fits here:** Two broadband simulation papers exist for NE India, but there is no 3-D velocity model of the Assam valley or the Sylhet trough — among the deepest sediment basins on Earth. Empirical GMPEs cannot capture basin resonance at those thicknesses; Guwahati, Silchar and Sylhet sit on it.
- **What you need:** Receiver functions, gravity and existing well logs to build the model; HPC for the simulation.
- **Read these:**
    - Graves et al. 2010 — *Pure and Applied Geophysics* — [10.1007/s00024-010-0161-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00024-010-0161-6)

## 13. Full-waveform / adjoint tomography

**Track:** Seismology · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** compute

- **Why this rank:** The science case is strong — slab geometry below 150 km is still argued over — but the HPC allocation is the gate, and the groups competing already have one.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Now routine for Japan, Europe and North America (Fichtner, Tape and successors).
- **Why it fits here:** Every tomographic image of the Indo-Burma slab to date is ray-based travel-time inversion. Slab geometry below ~150 km is still argued over — Rao & Kumar's cessation hypothesis versus continued subduction. Waveform inversion resolves what ray theory smears.
- **What you need:** Waveform archive plus HPC time. The bottleneck is compute, not data.
- **Read these:**
    - Tape et al. 2009 — *Science* — [10.1126/science.1175298](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1175298)
    - Fichtner et al. 2009 — *Geophysical Journal International* — [10.1111/j.1365-246x.2009.04368.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-246x.2009.04368.x)

## 14. Distributed acoustic sensing on telecom dark fibre

**Track:** Seismology · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** field

- **Why this rank:** Technically the cheapest dense array available in this region, but blocked on a dark-fibre agreement and an interrogator. That is an institutional negotiation, not a technical problem.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Ajo-Franklin (2019), Sladen (2019) and Williams (2019) — all three sit in your library — turned unused telecom fibre into thousands of seismic channels.
- **Why it fits here:** A CrossRef search returns zero DAS seismology papers for India. Fibre already runs along the highways and rail across the Shillong Plateau and up the Assam valley. It is dense-array sampling without vaults, permits or power.
- **What you need:** An interrogator unit (rentable) and a dark-fibre agreement with a telecom operator. The single cheapest route to dense data here.
- **Read these:**
    - Ajo-Franklin et al. 2019 — *Scientific Reports* — [10.1038/s41598-018-36675-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-36675-8)
    - Sladen et al. 2019 — *Nature Communications* — [10.1038/s41467-019-13793-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13793-z)

## 15. Dense nodal (large-N) array deployments

**Track:** Seismology · **Status:** one study · **Cost:** field

- **Why this rank:** A funded field season and 100–300 rented nodes. Excellent second-half-of-PhD work once money exists; impossible before it.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Thousands-of-node deployments are now routine in California and Oklahoma. Regionally there is exactly one — a 2025 site-amplification study in Yangon.
- **Why it fits here:** Three-to-six-week nodal deployments across the Kopili fault, the Dauki front or the Shillong Plateau margin would give basin depth, fault location and site response at a spatial density no permanent network can reach.
- **What you need:** 100–300 rentable nodes and a few weeks of fieldwork. No vaults, no telemetry, batteries only.
- **Read these:**
    - Ben-Zion et al. 2015 — *Geophysical Journal International* — [10.1093/gji/ggv142](https://doi.org/10.1093/gji/ggv142)

## 16. Palaeoseismic trenching on the fold-belt faults

**Track:** Hazard · **Status:** partial · **Cost:** field

- **Why this rank:** The only route to pre-instrumental recurrence on Kopili and Churachandpur–Mao, but it is permits, field seasons, radiocarbon budgets and a different discipline.
- **Proven elsewhere:** The Himalayan front has a dense trenching record establishing recurrence for great earthquakes.
- **Why it fits here:** Shillong and Dauki have some palaeoseismic control (Bilham & England 1999; Sukhija 1999), but the Churachandpur–Mao and Kopili faults — the structures now shown to be creeping and seismically active — have no trench-derived recurrence at all.
- **What you need:** Field seasons, trenching permits and radiocarbon dating. Slow, but it is the only source of pre-instrumental recurrence.
- **Read these:**
    - Bilham et al. 2001 — *Nature* — [10.1038/35071057](https://doi.org/10.1038/35071057)
    - Sukhija et al. 1999 — *Earth and Planetary Science Letters* — [10.1016/s0012-821x(99)00015-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0012-821x(99)00015-1)

## 17. Ocean-bottom seismometers on the Rakhine–Bengal margin

**Track:** Seismology · **Status:** none here · **Cost:** major

- **Why this rank:** The most consequential item on the list and the least reachable alone: ship time, an OBS pool and an international consortium. A proposal to join, not to lead.
- **Proven elsewhere:** Cascadia, Hikurangi and Sumatra all resolved their updip megathrust only after going offshore.
- **Why it fits here:** Cummins (2007, Nature) argued the northern Bay of Bengal can host a giant tsunamigenic earthquake. The updip, offshore part of that megathrust — the part that would generate the tsunami — is sampled by no instrument. Onshore networks cannot resolve it.
- **What you need:** A ship, an OBS pool and international collaboration. The most expensive item here and the most consequential.
- **Read these:**
    - Cummins 2007 — *Nature* — [10.1038/nature06088](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06088)
    - Wallace et al. 2016 — *Science* — [10.1126/science.aaf2349](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf2349)
